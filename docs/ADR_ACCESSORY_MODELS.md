# Architecture Decision Record: Body Capabilities and Accessory Models

* **Status:** Accepted. Implemented (body side and tooling). The Quaternius Universal Base Characters and Modular Outfits (CC0) are installed. No pre-modeled accessory files (`assets/wardrobe/<socket>/<item>.glb`) are installed yet (the install path was tried end to end with a test hat, in a scratch deploy); the built-in parametric accessories (glasses, hats, belts...) work as before.
* **Date:** 2026-11-02
* **Deciders:** Architecture & Core Engineering Team
* **Related:** [SPEC_BLENDER.md](SPEC_BLENDER.md) (3-tier templates, wardrobe sockets), [ADR_IMPORTED_LOOK_LOCK.md](ADR_IMPORTED_LOOK_LOCK.md), [ADR_GENERATED_BODIES.md](ADR_GENERATED_BODIES.md), [ADR_PERSONALITY_PROFILES.md](ADR_PERSONALITY_PROFILES.md)

## 1. Context

The look catalog is global: every accessory is offered for every body. That was
fine while every body was procedural. Real models change two things:

* An installed body (Tier 1 `custom:` or Tier 2 `standard_humanoid_*`) has its own rig. It may have no hands, no neck, or no head bone at all (a mascot rigged as a trunk with arms and legs, say). An accessory for a socket it cannot carry would float in the air.
* Pre-modeled accessory models (`assets/wardrobe/<socket>/<item>.glb`) existed, but were placed at a fixed point near the head, were not bound to the body's skeleton, and were offered to every body.

Garments (outfits, footwear) are generated from the procedural body's shape and cannot be fitted onto an arbitrary mesh, so installed bodies keep their own clothes. The exception is a garment that was *made for the body's own skeleton*, like the modular outfit packs sold beside a base character: it needs no fitting, only re-binding (decision 7).

## 2. Decision

**What a body can carry is read from its rig, not listed by hand.**

1. **Bone mapping (`blender/sockets.py`).** Each socket needs a role: head, neck, chest, waist, or a hand. The rig's bones are matched on meaning, not on one naming scheme (Mixamo `mixamorig:RightHand`, Blender `hand.R`, `Hand_R`...). A socket is carried only if the rig has the bone for it. Finger bones never count as hands. A rig with no torso bones at all can use a bone that names itself the body (`Body`, `Torso`...) for the head and torso sockets; a root or limb bone is never guessed. A model with no rig is given the generator's canonical rig (`root`, `spine`, `head`), so it carries those sockets.
2. **Inspection.** `generator.py --inspect` writes `socket_bones` and `supported_sockets` beside the existing capabilities. `python -m brain.appearance.models add-body` installs the file, inspects it and stores the result as `<model>.caps.json` next to it.
3. **Accessory sidecars.** An accessory `<item>.glb` may have a `<item>.json` with `requires` (body capabilities it needs; defaults from its socket), `scale`, `offset` (metres) and `rotation` (radians). With no sidecar it still works at natural size. The model's origin is its attach point.
4. **The catalog is computed, not edited.** `effective_wardrobe_catalog(deploy_dir, base_template)` takes the wardrobe catalog, adds every accessory model on disk, removes sockets the body cannot carry and models whose requirements it does not meet, and offers an installed body no outfits or footwear. Validation and the designer's prompt both use it, so the model is only offered what works and an invalid choice is rejected with the list of what does.
5. **Placement and binding on an installed body.** The socket's location comes from the bones and the figure's bounds (glasses from the model's own eyes, centred on them and sized by their spacing when it has an eye mesh; other head sockets from the top of the figure and a ray cast onto the face; chest, waist and hands from their bones). Accessories are attached rigidly to the socket's bone (weight 1.0 on one bone), so they follow animation. Parametric items, drawn for the procedural bodies' large head, are scaled by the head's size relative to that; items that fit themselves to the body (belt, badge, backpack) are not.
6. **Unknown means permissive.** A model installed before this existed has no `.caps.json`. Its sockets are treated as unknown and everything is allowed, as before, until it is inspected.

7. **Outfit models.** `assets/outfit_models/<body>/<outfit>.glb` (`<body>` is the base template id with `:` written `_`, e.g. `standard_humanoid_female`, `custom_robot`) holds skinned meshes made for that body's skeleton. The designer is offered them as `wardrobe.outfit` values on that body only. To wear one the generator re-binds its meshes to the body's armature (so they follow every pose) and deletes the body's vertices whose strongest weight is not the head or neck, since the outfit supplies the rest, hands and feet included; left in, the body clips through the cloth. An outfit with bones the body lacks, or no rig, is skipped with a warning. Belts, badges and pockets then fit the worn torso. Footwear stays unavailable on an installed body (an outfit brings its own). A generic catalog garment (`hoodie`) still validates on an installed body, as before, and is ignored with a warning, so a delta that switches a procedural look to an installed body does not fail.
8. **Conversion at install.** A model is often shipped as `.gltf` with 2K-4K PNG textures and more detail than the budgets (30,000 triangles, 15 MB per build) allow. `add-body` and `add-outfit` accept `.gltf`; the generator's `--convert` mode re-exports it as one `.glb` with textures shrunk (default 1024 px) and saved as JPEG unless they need alpha. `add-outfit` also decimates to about 18,000 triangles (`--max-triangles`; the body's head and eyes take about 7,000 and accessories a couple of thousand), which keeps bone weights.

9. **Lip-sync and blinking for an installed body with no mouth shape keys.** The generator already gives such a body a face overlay from a `<model>.face.json` sidecar (SPEC_BLENDER, "face overlay"): skin-coloured lids that close, and a nine-viseme mouth. `generator.py --face-landmarks` now writes that sidecar from the model's geometry, and `add-body` runs it (and `face-sidecar <body>` re-runs it; an existing sidecar is never overwritten without `--force`, since it may be hand-made). The eyes are the mesh whose material or texture is named for them (a pair, small, near the top of the figure, else the model is refused: a whole-body material that merely has "eye" in its name is not a face). The mouth is the lip crease in the face's side profile, the first local maximum after the upper-lip bulge, trusted only within the usual 55-75% of the way from eyes to chin and otherwise placed at 66.7%. For a realistic face the sidecar says `"patch": false` (keep the real lips; the mouth is drawn over them, since the default skin patch would erase them) and `"oval": true` (a round-edged dark mouth instead of a rectangle, with a strip of upper teeth along its top edge when it is open enough or the viseme is a teeth-together one). Landmarks are fractions of the body's own box (`body_bounds`), so an outfit worn over it (a hood) does not shift them.
10. **Gestures and skeletal idles on an installed body.** One mesh for the whole body used to get run poses but no arm gestures, because a mesh with run targets was skipped for gestures. It now gets the four gesture targets after the two run targets, and the overlay recognises a mesh with six targets as that pair (SPEC_OVERLAY). A rig with a head bone also gets idle, nod and shake clips baked on its own bones (`skeletal_clips` in the build and manifest), which the overlay plays by name; the generator's canonical clips are used only for generated bodies and rigs with no head bone.
11. **Holding a model back.** `<model>.hold` beside an installed `custom:` model keeps it on disk but out of the designer's options, and `validate_against_catalogs` refuses it, until `models release`. Used for models whose licence has not been checked.

12. **Relaxed arms.** Character bases are usually modelled in a T-pose and keep it as their rest pose, so the avatar idled with its arms out, and the gestures (which swing a limb sideways from hanging) flapped it up and down instead. An upper arm whose downward component is below `RELAX_IF_HANGING_LESS_THAN` (0.75, which is more than about 41 degrees off vertical: a T-pose arm is 0, a raised or bent arm about 0.6) is brought down to `RELAXED_ARM_DEG` (10) off vertical, with the forearm `RELAXED_ELBOW_DEG` (5) forward, and that is committed as the rest pose, after any outfit is worn, so everything hangs together; accessories, bounds and the morph poses are all measured from it. Arms that already hang, or are held forward (a mech's, at 0.8 and above), are left as built. The running poses also swing the arms against the legs, for a body that was relaxed this way. The original gesture poses (the overlay blends each arm between its "out" and "in" pose about once a second) were tuned on a mascot whose forearm swings in the plane of the body; on a person that is an elbow hinging sideways, with the upper arm dragging the shoulder and torso skin. A relaxed body gets talking gestures instead, identical for both arms: the elbow bends forward (about 72 degrees out, 28 in) and the upper arm moves only a little. A test pins the forward reach, which the older poses lacked entirely.

13. **Per-model pose fixes.** `<model>.pose.json` beside an installed model, `{"roll_bones": {"<bone>": degrees}}`, turns a bone about its own length in the rest pose, before the arms are relaxed and before any morph pose is captured. A hand modelled with its fingers turned outward makes an arm that bends read as folding the wrong way; `{"<hand bone>": 180}` turns it. A bone the model lacks is a warning, not a failure. The same file may hold `"face_yaw": degrees`, which turns a model that does not face the overlay's front (-Y) about the vertical axis before it is scaled (a robot modelled facing +X takes `-90`, one facing away takes `180`). Written by hand: it is a correction to one model, not something to derive.

14. **Animation libraries.** A pack of clips made for a rig is installed with `models add-animations <file> --name <pack>` (into `assets/animation_library/`) and worn by installed bodies. Quaternius' Universal Animation Library is the supported source (decision 15).
    * **What fits.** Clips are keyed by bone name. A body wears a library if it has the core of the pack's bones (hips, both legs and both arms: `REQUIRED_BONES`). A body that lacks others (fingers, toes, clavicles, a spine joint) plays the clips without the tracks of the bones it lacks (`wear_animation_library` drops them) and is shown as having what stays still. A rig with other bone names ignores the library and keeps decisions 10 and 12.
    * **How it is worn.** The generator (`plan_animation_library`, `wear_animation_library`) moves the clips that fill a role onto the body's armature as they are. Rotations do not depend on proportions; only the hips' travel is scaled, by the ratio of the two hip heights, and no bone's length is touched. A body that wears a library keeps its own rest pose, because the clips are relative to it, so it is not relaxed (decision 12) and gets no morph-target run or gesture poses and no baked head idles, which the clips replace. Lip-sync and blinking are unaffected. The look's thumbnail is posed at the first frame of the idle clip, not in the T-pose.
    * **Roles.** Behaviours are named roles (idle, talk, run, walk, mood variations, gestures, sitting, sleeping, working out). Which clips fill each role, best first, is data in the pack profiles (decision 22), read by `blender/animation_library.py` (pure Python, shared by the generator and `models list`), and the build reports which clip fills which role as `library_clips` (a manifest field, sent to the overlay). A role can have more than one clip (a profile's `extras`, listed as `<role>.alt1`, `<role>.alt2`, each with its own `.low` / `.high` / `.mirror` copies), and a role that a second library also fills takes that library's clips as more options (`merge_picks`). The overlay picks one at random for each occasion. What the overlay does with each role is in SPEC_OVERLAY.
    * **Pace.** Idle clips can be played slower than made (a profile's `time_scale`), since a pack's idle may be livelier than reads as calm.
    * **Sitting on a chair.** The sit clips meant for a chair are played with a chair, which the generator builds from the posed body (`build_sit_prop`, named `SitProp`: a seat, four legs and a low back). It is sized and placed so that the body steps back onto it: the seat's top is where the lowest vertex under the hips is, and its front edge is just behind where the body stands when the sit-down begins. The overlay cannot find a node by name (the loader leaves them unnamed), so the chair carries three morph targets, a collapse to nothing and two fillers that make the count of three unique, and the controller drives the collapse weight (SPEC_OVERLAY). The chair is left out of the bounds and the thumbnail. A rig with no `pelvis`, `thigh_l` or `calf_l` to place it from gets no sit clips, with a warning, since it would sit on air. The three clips of a set (enter, idle, exit) come from one pack: sitting on the ground if the pack has it, else the chair set.
    * **Choosing clips.** A clip fills a role by how it looks in motion, not by its name; one that reads as something else than its name suggests is not used for that role.
    * **Listing.** `models list` shows, per body, which libraries fit its rig and what they give, and the status page's Animations tab shows the same.

15. **Quaternius is the supported source.** Bodies, outfits and animation libraries are made and tested for Quaternius content, and its bone names, T-pose rest pose and clip names are the convention everything else is matched against (see "Supported models" in SPEC_BLENDER). Content from another source needs two things before it works with the rest: an identifier (what kind of rig is this, from its bone names) and a converter to the Quaternius names and rest pose (for a body or an outfit: rename and re-skin; for a clip: retarget its rotations to the Quaternius bones). Until a source has both, its models are installed as `custom:` bodies with the generic, rig-reading features (decisions 1-12) and get no outfits or animation libraries. The per-body listing (`models list`) shows what each body can use.

16. **Mixamo converter.** Mixamo's skeleton has the same bones as the Quaternius one in the same arrangement (bar a tip bone and Quaternius' `root`) and the same T-pose, so it is converted, not rebuilt (`blender/mixamo_convert.py`; the name map and the clip processing are in `blender/animation_library.py`, pure Python).
    * **A body** (`add-body <file>.fbx`). The bones and vertex groups are renamed to the Quaternius names; each bone is rolled like the Quaternius bone (a clip turns a bone about its own axes, so a body that is only renamed crosses its legs under the clips); `root` is added above the hips; the FBX's centimetre scale and rotation are applied. The result then goes through the usual `add-body` steps.
    * **Clips** (`add-animations <files>.fbx --name <pack>`, which uses Blender's `wm.fbx_import`). Every frame is retargeted as the change in each bone's world rotation from its rest pose, the hips' travel is scaled by hip height, and the pack is written as an ordinary library (decision 14) with its keys thinned to 0.25 degrees. The glTF exporter turns a frame into a time as frame/fps, so every clip's first key is at frame 0: a clip that starts later shows the rest pose, a T-pose, until it does (`late_starts` reports one that does).
    * **Per-clip options** (a profile's `clips` rules, overridable with `--options`):
        * `trim` / `max_seconds`: the part of the clip wanted.
        * `loop`: the end eased back to the start.
        * `in_place`: the hips' sideways and forward travel taken out.
        * `variants`: a `_Low` and a `_High` copy (less and more movement, slower and faster, about the first pose), for how eager a gesture is.
        * `extract`: part of a clip as a clip of its own.
        * `mirror` (on by default): the clip made a second time with left and right swapped (`<clip>_Mirror`; each bone's world change from rest is reflected in the plane that splits the body and the hips' sideways travel flips), so a gesture is not always made with the same hand. A mirrored copy has no intensity variants of its own, which cost size; `mirror_main: false` mirrors only the extracted parts of a long clip.
        * `seat`: a seated clip is moved to where the sit clips seat the body and eased in from, and back to, the sit pose, so playing it from the sit loop does not jump. Its hips go the whole way to the seat height and its legs are re-solved with two-bone IK (`Host.plant_feet`) so the feet stay where the clip puts them (kept above the floor, the knee bending the way the clip bends it); `plant_feet: false` moves the hips only.
        * `ping_pong`: out and back along the same path, slowed by the factor given, with the time eased so the body is still at the turn: a movement that must end where it began.
        * `reverse`, and the joining options below.
    * **Intensity.** A Mixamo modifier (how enthusiastic a wave is) is baked into the file and the `.fbx` carries no trace of it, so the intensity variants are made here. The brain's `play_gesture` carries an `intensity` (0..1, from valence, energy and stress; 0.5 at rest) that the overlay turns into the low, normal or high clip.
    * **Joining clips** (`finish_clips`, pure Python). Clips that start or end in different poses are joined by easing, not cut. `ease_from` / `ease_to` ease a clip in from, or out to, the first pose of another (`@stand` is the standing idle's first pose, `stand_reference`), `blend_ref` does both ends to one pose, `ease_frames` says how long, `compose` joins clips one after another (a `[name, repeat]` pair repeats one) and `hold` eases to or from a pose held. Derived clips are made this way (a profile's `derived` recipes): a workout is lying down, the exercise, and getting up, from and to the standing idle; sitting on the floor is eased down from standing, the idle, and back up. They are `in_place`, so nothing travels and the next clip does not jump. A reference that the pack lacks is ignored.
    * **Turning on the floor.** Joining a push-up position to a pose lying with the head the other way is a turn of about 160 degrees of the pelvis. Eased with a slerp, which takes the shortest arc, the body goes head over heels, through a handstand. `lying_turn` (used by `ease_ends` and `join_clips`, for the pelvis only, when both poses have the body's length within 37 degrees of the floor, which the pelvis bone's rest orientation, `pelvis_rest` in the clip's metadata, tells) does it as a body on the floor does: a spin about the vertical for the first 70 % of the time and a roll about its own length from 30 % on (the roll is about the body, and the whole is then turned). The eases to and from the floor are 36 samples and a workout's joins 30 (about a second).
    * **A clip that loops badly.** A clip whose end is not its start is not looped, since the loop would undo the difference in a fraction of a second: the part wanted is cut (`max_seconds`) and made out and back (`ping_pong`), so it ends in the pose it began in. A clip that is another place the body goes to, not a loop, gets no role if easing it to the base pose would be another jump of that kind.
    * **Roles added:** greet (with `.low` / `.high`), angry, hip_thrust, sit_alt (a seated variation), sit_talk (a loop played while it speaks from the chair; it needs a chair, so the floor sit has none), workout, workout_floor, lie_down, sleep, sleep_alt, wake, wake_slow, idle_look, idle_sad, and the floor-sit set (floor_sit_enter, floor_sit_idle, floor_sit_exit: `hold` clips eased from the standing pose to the seated one and back, over 36 samples). `angry` and `hip_thrust` are also gestures the companion can do. SPEC_OVERLAY says when each is played.

17. **Sketchfab importer.** A Sketchfab download (a .glb whose `asset.generator` starts with `Sketchfab`) is the author's model re-exported by Sketchfab, and installing it as it comes does not work:
    * the author's scale sits on wrapper nodes above the model;
    * every node name has a uniqueness suffix (`CC_Base_Head_038`);
    * Blender's importer adds a helper bone above each scaled bone (`..._scaleCompensation`), so that the sockets land on the helpers;
    * the file carries the motion it was exported with;
    * a very high polygon count is normal, and the meshes have shape keys, which a mesh cannot be decimated with, so it would stay far over the build's triangle budget (decision 8);
    * the importer's own bone-display shape was re-exported with the model (a bug for any rigged model, fixed in `run_convert`).

    `add-body` therefore recognises a Sketchfab file and cleans it first (`blender/sketchfab_convert.py`, rules in `blender/sketchfab.py`, pure Python):
    * the wrapper transforms are baked into a one-metre-scale, Z-up skeleton and meshes with nothing above them;
    * the helper bones and `_rootJoint` are removed and the bones under them re-parented;
    * the suffix is taken off the bones and vertex groups (one suffix only; a name that would then be taken keeps Sketchfab's);
    * shape keys, the baked motion, the empties and the display shape are dropped;
    * loose parts hanging from one bone are skinned to it;
    * textures are shrunk (JPEG) and the meshes decimated to the body budget in the same pass (the small parts keep their detail, so the ratio is taken on the rest);
    * the file is made again with halved textures while it is over 8 MB.

    The cleaned file then goes through the usual `add-body` steps (inspection, sockets, face sidecar).

    **Licence.** The author, title, source and licence are in `asset.extras`. They are recorded in `<model>.credits.json` beside the installed model (for bodies, outfits and accessories), shown by `models list` and the status page's Animations tab, and what the licence asks is printed on install: CC-BY and CC-BY-SA need the author credited wherever the model is shown or shared, CC-BY-NC is for non-commercial use, CC-BY-ND forbids sharing a modified version (and everything is shrunk and re-rigged, so it is for the user's own use only), and a licence that is not Creative Commons is flagged to be read. Nothing is refused: the user decides. The models themselves are never part of the repository.

    **Scope.** This is an importer, not a skeleton converter. A Sketchfab rig is whatever its author made (Character Creator's `CC_Base_*`, Auto-Rig Pro's `*_stretch_l`...), so the Quaternius animation libraries do not fit it as it is (decision 15) and `models list` says so. Decision 18 converts the known rig families; a rig of no known family plays the generator's own motion for imported rigs (the hop and the baked limb poses, the head idles), for which the limb finder also knows Auto-Rig Pro's `thigh_stretch` / `leg_stretch` / `arm_stretch` / `forearm_stretch`.

18. **Rig converter.** A body of a known rig family plays the Quaternius animation libraries through a conversion (`blender/rig_convert.py`; the family detection and the bone maps are in `blender/rigs.py`, pure Python). Two families are known: Character Creator (`CC_Base_*`) and Auto-Rig Pro (`*_stretch_l`, `root_x`). Decision 16 renames Mixamo's bones one for one because its skeleton is the Quaternius one in all but names. These rigs are not:
    * their bones differ in number (more spine joints, twist and facial bones, a hips bone with a pelvis bone under it);
    * their bones are turned differently (a clip turns a bone about its own axes, and rolls differ from tool to tool);
    * their rest pose differs (Character Creator's arms hang, while the Quaternius clips are the change from a T-pose).

    So a library is not worn as it is: it is **retargeted** onto the body.

    **Body** (`add-body` does it for a `custom:` body of a known family, unless `--no-rig`):
    * the bones that have a Quaternius equivalent are renamed (`CC_Base_Hip` is `pelvis`, `CC_Base_Waist` `spine_01`, `arm_stretch_l` `upperarm_l`...; a rig with more spine joints gives the first, the middle and the last), and every other bone keeps its name and follows its parent;
    * the body is turned to face as the Quaternius characters do (its left along +X);
    * if its arms are not within 12 degrees of straight out, they are posed into a T-pose, the meshes deformed to it and the pose made the rest pose (so shape keys, which cannot be deformed, are gone: decision 17).

    **Libraries** (every installed library, when the body is installed; a library added later, when it is added):
    * each clip a role uses (`wanted_clips`, not the whole pack) has its frames walked on the library's skeleton, and the change of each bone's world rotation from its rest pose is applied to the body's bone as the change from its own, so the bones' own axes do not matter, only that both rest in the same pose, which the T-pose ensures;
    * a bone with no equivalent stays at its place under its parent;
    * the hips' travel is scaled by how tall the two hips stand;
    * the keys are thinned as the Mixamo converter does, and the first is at time 0 (decision 16).

    The result is the body's own library, `assets/animation_library_body/<body>/<library>.glb`, and `<body>.rig.json` says the body is converted: the generator (`plan_animation_library`, `library_folder`) then plays those libraries, not the shared ones, and `models list` says so. A body lacking Quaternius bones plays them without those tracks (decision 14).

    **Not covered:**
    * A rig of another family has no map (`rig_family` says none): the body is installed as it is and plays the generator's own motion.
    * A skeleton whose bones are not named for what they are (`Bone.001`) cannot be mapped by name.
    * A retargeted clip does not account for different limb proportions: the feet may slide or sink a little on a body with short legs, and the hand may not reach where the clip's does.
    * The seat of a chair-sit clip is the library's, which may sit a body of other proportions a little high or low on its chair.

19. **Model bundles (export and import).** The models are in the deploy directory, on the machine's own disk, and nothing but the nightly rsync of the whole directory (`deploy/backup_state.sh`) copies them anywhere. A second install (another operating system, or a machine with no shared storage that is fed from a thumb drive) needs the models alone, carried as one file, in a form that cannot half-arrive. Zipping the directory does not do that: it takes the live database (copied mid-write it can be torn; the backup script uses SQLite's backup API for that reason), the platform's binaries in `tools/`, the built looks and the voices, which are not models, and it checks nothing on the other side. `models export <file.zip>` and `models import <file.zip>` (`brain/appearance/bundle.py`, standard library only: no Blender to export) do it.
    * **What is in a bundle.** The files under `assets/` in `base_templates`, `custom_templates`, `outfit_models`, `wardrobe` (accessories), `animation_library` and `animation_library_body` (the libraries retargeted onto converted bodies, decision 18), restricted to `.glb`, `.blend` and `.json` and never a `.hold` marker (state). Each model's sockets, face, pose, rig and credits sidecars go with it.
    * **What is not.** The database, the look history and built looks, voices, tools, and the catalogs (the repository seeds them, so they come with it; an export says so if they differ from the seed). A bundle therefore makes a *fresh companion* with the models and nothing of the old one.
    * **The format** (`bundle.json`, format 1): a version, when it was made, whether it includes the derived libraries, the Blender and repository revision it was made with (for diagnosis only), the models by kind, and `files`: `{path: {sha256, size}}`, the map that a remote worker's file transfer needs too (ADR_REMOTE_MODEL_PROCESSING). A `CREDITS.txt` lists every credited model with its licence and what it asks (attribution, non-commercial, no derivatives), so a bundle that is passed on carries its obligations, and an import prints them.
    * **Export** refuses what could not be a file on Windows (a reserved name such as `CON`, a character `<>:"\|?*`, a trailing dot or space, a path of more than 150 characters, two names that differ only in case) and writes to a temporary file that replaces the target only when complete. It warns when the bundle would not fit a FAT32 drive (4 GB).
    * **Import** trusts nothing in the bundle. Every name must be a relative path under a model folder with a model suffix (no `..`, no drive, no backslash, nothing outside `assets/<model folder>/`); every file must be in the manifest and every manifest entry in the bundle; sizes must match; the total unpacked size is capped (8 GB); and every file is unpacked to a staging directory and checked against its hash before anything is moved into place, so a damaged or altered bundle writes nothing. A file already there with the same contents is left; one with different contents is a conflict and is left too, unless `--replace`; a name that differs only in case from one there is a conflict too (a Windows install cannot hold both). `--dry-run` reads and verifies the whole bundle and writes nothing.
    * **Derived libraries** (the retargeted ones) are in the bundle by default, so that a machine without Blender can use its converted bodies at once. `export --no-derived` leaves them out (they are most of the size) and `import --regenerate` makes them again (Blender needed, as for `add-animations`; use it when the Blender version differs from the one that made them). An import of a bundle with a converted body and no libraries for it says so.
    * **A look to start with** (`export --include-look [--look-version N]`). The models alone give an install nothing to show until Blender has built a look from them, and a machine may not have Blender yet. The bundle can therefore also carry one finished look: its built `.glb` (checked against its record's hash at export, so a damaged or pruned file stops the export), its manifest record, and its picture, as the three files `look/look.glb`, `look/entry.json` and `look/look.png` (no other name is accepted under `look/`). An import into an install that has **no `manifest.json`** sets it up as a first publish does, with the version number reset: the file into `assets/` and `data/current/`, the picture into `looks/`, then the manifest with that one record as **version 1 and the current look**. The companion starts with it and the overlay plays it (its clips, outfit and poses are in the file, so nothing needs Blender to be seen); the next look is version 2, built by Blender once it is installed and configured. An install that already has looks is left alone (the bundle's look is reported as not used): an import never rewrites a companion's history. A bad look (a file that is not the one its record describes, a record that is not a look record) is refused before anything is written. The look carried is a build of the models in the same bundle, so what it was made from is there for the next build.
    * **Not covered:** the companion's memory and personality (`state.db`; a deliberate choice for a fresh companion, and `backup_state.sh` already snapshots it), the rest of the look history (the other looks rebuild from their specs), voices (a companion's own), and the port to another operating system itself (Blender's path, the scripts and the overlay build are separate work).

20. **Unweighted parts.** A part that heat-map weighting leaves unweighted (loose low-poly pieces, common in downloaded robots) is bound whole to the bone nearest its centre, instead of being exported detached from the body.

21. **Native clips.** A tool such as Mesh2Motion exports a clip, or a few, on the Quaternius skeleton itself (the same bone names): `add-animations <files>.glb --retarget --name <pack>`, which may be given `.fbx` files too, converts them. They need no renaming, but a plain library install is wrong for them:
    * the file's own rest pose is the pose it was exported in (a bow, a seat on the floor), not the skeleton's rest, so the generator's scaling of the hips' travel by the library's rest hip height would be wrong for them;
    * a clip's first key may be a frame late, and the body shows the rest pose until it (decision 16);
    * nothing joins a standing clip to the idle, or a floor clip to the standing ones.

    `Host.retarget_native` therefore reads them in Blender without measuring anything from the file's rest: each bone's *world orientation* in each frame is the orientation given to the same bone on the host body (the two skeletons share the bones' frames, since both were imported the same way), the hips' height is scaled by the ratio of the two leg lengths, and the first sample is the clip's first key. Checked by cutting two clips out of a library whose rest pose is true, converting them this way and comparing with the originals: the idle came back within 0.1 degrees on average, the walk within 3 degrees at worst and the hips' positions were identical. The clips are then processed and joined as the Mixamo clips are (decision 16: easing to the standing idle, loops, derived clips), and are not mirrored.
    * **A clip that is only a building block** has the option `emit: false`: it is made, so that others can be built from it, but not written. A pack can use a clip of another source this way (the lie-down and get-up of a face-up sleep, and the push-up workout, are built from the push-up position that a Mixamo clip starts in), and `--options` gives it.
    * **Credit.** `--credits <file.json>` keeps a `<pack>.credits.json` ({title, author, source, license}) beside the library, as for a model (decision 17). Mesh2Motion's clips are CC0.
    * **Sets.** A way of sleeping (the clip that lies down into it, the loop, a shift in it, the get-up out of it) and a way of sitting on the floor (enter, idle, exit) are sets: each clip begins and ends in the pose the next needs, so the overlay must play a whole set. The manifest lists them as option N of each role, and `merge_picks` numbers the roles of a set (`PAIRED_ROLES`) together, by how many sets there already are, so that option N of each is the same set whichever library has which and in whatever order. A role that a set lacks is left empty at that number (a way of sleeping may have no shift), and the overlay plays what the set it picked has.
    * **Roles.** Native clips add `attention` (a raised arm, both arms up: it tries to get the user's attention), `think_long` (a hand to the head: it does not know the answer), a bow as another way to greet, a way to work out (push-ups), a way to sleep (face up) and a way to sit on the floor (cross-legged, meditating). SPEC_OVERLAY says when each is played.

22. **Pack profiles.** What the clips of an animation pack are for, and how to convert them, is data and not code: the engine (`blender/animation_library.py`, `mixamo_convert.py`, the generator) knows roles and mechanisms, never a particular pack's clip names, so a pack it has never seen needs a file and no change to the code. A profile (`blender/pack_profiles.py`) is a JSON file that knows clips by name:
    * `roles`: role to the clips that can fill it, best first (the first one present is the role's clip), and `extras`: role to more clips, played as options (decision 14).
    * `clips`: conversion rules, `{match, options}`, where `match` is a clip name, a glob (`*Greeting*`) or a list of them and `options` are those of decision 16 (`loop`, `variants`, `extract`, `ping_pong`, `ease_from`...); every rule that matches applies, in order, so a later rule refines an earlier one.
    * `derived`: clips made of others, `{name, requires, options}`: made when every clip in `requires` is in the pack (`compose`, `hold` and the easing options of decision 16).
    * `sit_sets`: the ways of sitting down (enter, idle, exit; `chair: true` for the ones that need a chair, decision 14) and `time_scale`: how much slower than made a role is played.
    * `disable`: clips not to use for any role.

    **Layers.** One profile ships, in `blender/packs/`: `quaternius.json`, the supported source, always first: its clips fill the base roles and its sit sets. Every other pack is the user's: its profile goes in `<deploy_dir>/assets/animation_profiles/*.json`, which is the install's and not the repository's (`models add-profile <file>`; they are in the export bundles, decision 19), because which clips of a pack fill which role is a choice about that user's own downloads. A role's clips are the union of the layers with the user's first, so their preference wins; conversion rules apply in layer order with the user's last, so theirs win; a derived clip is replaced by name; `disable` takes a clip out of every role. Clip names are what ties a profile to a pack, so a profile for a pack that is not installed does nothing, and a checkout with only the Quaternius libraries behaves as it did before any other source was supported. The tests use example packs as test data, loaded as an install's own profiles.

    **Checks.** A profile is validated when it is loaded: the format, the name, the sections, that every role is one the overlay knows (`KNOWN_ROLES`), that every conversion option is one the converter understands, that a derived clip is composed or held, and the shape of the sit sets. A profile that does not validate is skipped with every reason, once, on stderr, and never stops the rest or the build; `models check-profile <file>` says what is wrong before it is installed, and `models profiles` lists the layers in use and what was skipped. A profile that changes on disk is read again (the status page's process lives longer than a build). The roles themselves are not data: they are the overlay's behaviours, and a new one is code (SPEC_OVERLAY).

23. **A model with no skeleton: rigging by parts.** Many cartoon and mech models have no skeleton but are made of separate pieces (a head, a torso, a limb of several pieces). Rigging them with a skinning tool does not work (an automatic fit to a human skeleton smears limbs into the body when the model is not a human in a T-pose), and a rigid piece needs no skinning: it follows one bone. `models rig-body <file.glb> --name custom:<name>` (`blender/part_rig.py`) does this for a `.glb` of at least six separate parts:
    * **What each part is** comes from where its centre is, as fractions of the figure's height (the figure is first turned `--yaw` degrees to face -Y, its left on +X; the default is the `face_yaw` of a `<file>.pose.json` beside it): the head in the top fifth near the centre line, the torso above 55 % near it, an arm above 30 % and further out than `--arm-offset` (0.14, or 0.09 for arms that hang close to the body), a leg the rest, by side. The height is taken from the second lowest and second highest part, so a stray piece does not set it. A figure that cannot be told apart this way (no head, or no arms found) is refused, saying which and what to try.
    * **The skeleton** is the Quaternius bones a body has (root, pelvis, three spine bones, neck, head, and per side a clavicle, upper arm, lower arm, hand, thigh, calf, foot), placed on the figure's own joints. An arm is cut in three at the elbow, found as the point furthest from the line between its shoulder and its hand, and where its last part begins (the hand); a leg is cut at its middle and above its foot.
    * **The rest pose** is the T-pose and the standing legs the libraries are measured from (decision 18), so each chain is turned rigidly about its joints into it (an arm out to its side, a leg straight down, a foot as it stood), and the figure stands on the ground, centred, as the Quaternius characters do.
    * **Every part is bound whole to one bone** (one mesh per bone), so nothing stretches or clips.
    * **Options.** `--limbless` is for a figure with no arms or legs (a robot on wheels): the head (above 60 % of the height), the neck (40-60 %) and the body (the rest, wheels included, on the pelvis) are told apart, and the arm and leg bones are still made, at the size a figure of that height would have them, with nothing bound to them: the libraries need those bones (`REQUIRED_BONES`), and the head and torso motion of every clip plays, so a nod, a sway or a lean shows and a walk is a wheeled bob. `--split-loose` first cuts each mesh into its loose parts (coincident vertices merged first, because a flat-shaded export gives every face its own), for a model whose parts were exported as one mesh.
    The result is installed like any body, marked as converted (`<name>.rig.json`) and the libraries are retargeted onto it (decision 18), so it plays every clip the libraries have. `--replace` swaps one that is installed (see Consequences). **Scope:** it takes parts the classification can tell apart; a model that is one mesh (a blob, a single-piece robot) cannot be split this way and is not helped, and a model that is already skinned (such as a Sketchfab download with limb rigs of its own) is better adapted by naming its bones, which is model-specific work and not part of this command. The quality limit is mechanical: a joint where parts do not overlap shows a gap when it turns, as a robot's wrist does.

24. **Rig maps: a skeleton with a name map is a family that is data.** A skeleton whose bones are the Quaternius bones under other names does not need code: `blender/rig_maps/<name>.json` ({"format": 1, "name", "signature": [bones that must all be present], "absent": [bones that must not be], "bones": {source: Quaternius name}, "prune": false}, validated by `rigs.load_rig_map`) is read by `rigs.py` beside the two coded families (Character Creator, Auto-Rig Pro), and the conversion of decision 18 (`add-body` runs it, with the libraries retargeted onto the result) does the rest: the names, the facing, the T-pose arms. A target two bones would get is given to neither, so a map names one bone for each Quaternius bone, and two skeletons that differ there (a leg with a middle segment and one without) are two maps told apart by `absent`. A skeleton that lacks a bone the libraries cannot do without (`REQUIRED_BONES`: no arms, say) has nothing to play them with and is not mapped. Shipped: maps for the Quaternius Animated Mech Pack (the supported source) and for skeleton families that are widespread and need no other knowledge of a model: Rigify-like names, the Source-engine rig, and a plain toon-character naming. A map for a skeleton that only one model has belongs with that model and not in the repository.

    `"prune": true` folds the bones that have no Quaternius name into their nearest kept ancestor (their vertex weights are added to its, their children move up, the bones are removed). Every clip is baked for every bone, so a skeleton of well over a hundred bones (extra bones for parts that break off, for cloth, for props) makes a look too big and too slow to build; folded, the parts are still there and are no longer animated.
    `"fold": {source bone: Quaternius bone}` folds named bones into a bone that is kept (their vertex weights are added to its and they are removed): for a control bone that has a mesh weighted to it and that no library drives, such as the IK feet of the three-legged mech, which would otherwise stay on the ground while the legs move.
    `"arm_spread": degrees` (0 to 60) turns the upper arms outward by that much in every clip of the body's own library, written into the clips when a look is built (`wear_animation_library`). The libraries hang the arms straight down; a body whose legs or hips are wider than its shoulders, as the two-legged mech's are, has them go through its legs.
    A look build also leaves out the gestures (attention, greeting, celebrate, angry) that carry a part of the body out of the overlay window, measured through the overlay's own camera framing (`drop_clips_leaving_the_frame`): a long-armed body throwing both arms overhead is cut off at the window's edge. A body left without an attention clip asks for attention with the greeting that reaches furthest and fits.

    **Limits.** The conversion reads a Sketchfab file through `sketchfab_convert.py` (decision 17). That step stands up a figure that is bound lying down and is upright only in the first frame of its animation (the importer throws the animation away and so would keep the lying bind pose, seen edge-on): when the posed skinned meshes are clearly upright (taller than deep), the bound ones are not, and they are most of the figure, the pose is made the rest pose. A file made of several skeletons at different scales is not handled by this and comes out wrong.

## 3. Consequences

* A body's skin weights can be wider than its parts move. A rigid part (a disc, a plate) that a tool weighted partly to the shoulders and thighs beside it costs nothing while those bones stand still, and is dragged out of shape as soon as a library moves them. `models meshes <body>` lists the skinned meshes of an installed body with the bones that move them most, `models rebind-mesh <body> --mesh <name> --bone <bone>` binds one wholly to one bone, and `--rename <name>` gives a mesh a name that says what it is (a download's are often `Object_72`); only the mesh named is changed (`blender/rebind.py`).
* Adding a model is: `add-body` or `add-accessory`, then it appears in the designer's options only where it works. `python -m brain.appearance.models list` shows the matrix.
* An accessory for a socket the rig lacks is rejected at validation, with the options, instead of being built in the wrong place. If a socket has no bone at build time (a body never inspected), the item is placed approximately and a warning is recorded.
* Chest, waist and hand placement on unusual bodies is approximate. A sidecar `offset`/`scale` corrects an individual accessory.
* A rigged accessory `.glb` is flattened to a rigid piece in its rest pose; `add-accessory` warns about it. Static meshes are best.
* `.caps.json` is a snapshot. To change a body's file use `add-body <file> --name <name> --replace`: `add-body` refuses a name that is installed, and `--replace` deletes the old model, its sidecars (`.caps`, `.credits`, `.face`, `.pose`, `.rig`, `.hold`) and the libraries retargeted onto it only once the new file has been converted, so a failed conversion leaves the old body, and a sidecar written for the old file (a facing, a face) cannot be applied to the new one. `remove-body <name> [--dry-run] [--force]` deletes a body the same way (it lists what goes; it refuses the body of the look the companion wears now unless `--force`, because the next look could not use it). Outfits made for the body and the other models are not touched.
* An outfit is tied to one body: a female and a male outfit are two files under two folders, installed with the same name to be offered under it on each. Decimation and JPEG are lossy; keep the original download to re-install at other settings.
* The drawn mouth is a flat overlay: on a realistic face it reads as an open mouth at desktop-pet size, but it does not deform the lips or show teeth and tongue. Real mouth shape keys need topology that is cut at the lip line, which the free base meshes do not have. `add-body` prints the sidecar result; check the face in a render, since the mouth height is a proportion, not a measurement.
* An accessory placed over an outfit (a belt on a tunic) is fitted to the outfit's torso, and may be hidden by the outfit's own belts.

## 4. Alternatives considered

* **A hand-maintained compatibility table per body.** Goes stale the moment a model is added, and needs the user to know bone names.
* **Fitting generated garments onto an arbitrary installed body.** Needs per-mesh garment fitting; out of scope. Garments made for the body's own skeleton are supported (decision 7); other installed bodies keep their clothes.
* **Automatic weights for accessories.** Smears a hat across neighbouring bones. Rigid binding to one bone is exact.
* **The clip tables in Python.** Role-to-clip tables, per-clip options and derived recipes written as code (as they were first) mean that supporting another pack is a code change and that the engine carries the names of packs most installs do not have. Profiles (decision 22) keep the engine free of them.
* **All the pack knowledge private.** Shipping only the Quaternius clips and leaving every other pack to the user's own profiles would also keep the repository free of names, but someone with the same public Mixamo or Mesh2Motion clips would get nothing without writing the profile themselves. The profiles for those two are shipped, and the user's layer sits over them.

## 5. Open questions

* Hand sockets assume the hand bone's origin is at the wrist; a grip offset may be needed per item (the sidecar `offset` covers it).
* The pack's modular parts (hood, pauldrons, arms, legs, feet) could be offered separately and mixed; today only whole outfits are installed.
* Shared skin textures: an outfit carries a copy of the body's skin texture for its hands, which adds about 0.5 MB per build.
