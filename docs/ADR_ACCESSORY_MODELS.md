# Architecture Decision Record: Body Capabilities and Accessory Models

* **Status:** Accepted. Implemented (body side and tooling). The Quaternius Universal Base Characters and Modular Outfits (CC0) are installed. No pre-modeled accessory files (`assets/wardrobe/<socket>/<item>.glb`) are installed yet (the install path was tried end to end with a test hat, in a scratch deploy); the built-in parametric accessories (glasses, hats, belts...) work as before.
* **Date:** 2026-11-02
* **Deciders:** Architecture & Core Engineering Team
* **Related:** [SPEC_BLENDER.md](SPEC_BLENDER.md) (3-tier templates, wardrobe sockets), [ADR_IMPORTED_LOOK_LOCK.md](ADR_IMPORTED_LOOK_LOCK.md), [ADR_GENERATED_BODIES.md](ADR_GENERATED_BODIES.md), [ADR_PERSONALITY_PROFILES.md](ADR_PERSONALITY_PROFILES.md)

---

## 1. Context

The look catalog is global: every accessory is offered for every body. That was
fine while every body was procedural. Real models change two things:

* An installed body (Tier 1 `custom:` or Tier 2 `standard_humanoid_*`) has its own rig. It may have no hands, no neck, or no head bone at all (a mascot rigged as a trunk with arms and legs, say). An accessory for a socket it cannot carry would float in the air.
* Pre-modeled accessory models (`assets/wardrobe/<socket>/<item>.glb`) existed, but were placed at a fixed point near the head, were not bound to the body's skeleton, and were offered to every body.

Garments (outfits, footwear) are generated from the procedural body's shape and cannot be fitted onto an arbitrary mesh, so installed bodies keep their own clothes. The exception is a garment that was *made for the body's own skeleton*, like the modular outfit packs sold beside a base character: it needs no fitting, only re-binding (decision 7).

## 2. Decision

**What a body can carry is read from its rig, not listed by hand.**

1. **Bone mapping (`blender/sockets.py`).** Each socket needs a role: head, neck, chest, waist, or a hand. The rig's bones are matched on meaning, not on one naming scheme (Mixamo `mixamorig:RightHand`, Blender `hand.R`, `Hand_R`...). A socket is carried only if the rig has the bone for it. Finger bones never count as hands. A rig with no torso bones at all can use a bone that names itself the body (`Body`, `Torso`...) for the head and torso sockets; a root or limb bone is never guessed. A model with no rig is given the generator's canonical rig (`root`, `spine`, `head`), so it carries those sockets.
2. **Inspection.** `generator.py --inspect` writes `socket_bones` and `supported_sockets` beside the existing capabilities. `python -m brain.appearance.models add-body` installs the file, inspects it and stores the result as `<model>.caps.json` next to it.
3. **Accessory sidecars.** An accessory `<item>.glb` may have a `<item>.json` with `requires` (body capabilities it needs; defaults from its socket), `scale`, `offset` (metres) and `rotation` (radians). With no sidecar it still works at natural size. The model's origin is its attach point.
4. **The catalog is computed, not edited.** `effective_wardrobe_catalog(deploy_dir, base_template)` takes the wardrobe catalog, adds every accessory model on disk, removes sockets the body cannot carry and models whose requirements it does not meet, and offers an installed body no outfits or footwear. Validation and the designer's prompt both use it, so the model is only offered what works and an invalid choice is rejected with the list of what does.
5. **Placement and binding on an installed body.** The socket's location comes from the bones and the figure's bounds (glasses from the model's own eyes, centred on them and sized by their spacing when it has an eye mesh; other head sockets from the top of the figure and a ray cast onto the face; chest, waist and hands from their bones). Accessories are attached rigidly to the socket's bone (weight 1.0 on one bone), so they follow animation. Parametric items, drawn for the procedural bodies' large head, are scaled by the head's size relative to that; items that fit themselves to the body (belt, badge, backpack) are not.
6. **Unknown means permissive.** A model installed before this existed has no `.caps.json`. Its sockets are treated as unknown and everything is allowed, as before, until it is inspected.

7. **Outfit models.** `assets/outfit_models/<body>/<outfit>.glb` (`<body>` is the base template id with `:` written `_`, e.g. `standard_humanoid_female`, `custom_robot`) holds skinned meshes made for that body's skeleton. The designer is offered them as `wardrobe.outfit` values on that body only. To wear one the generator re-binds its meshes to the body's armature (so they follow every pose) and deletes the body's vertices whose strongest weight is not the head or neck, since the outfit supplies the rest, hands and feet included; left in, the body clips through the cloth. An outfit with bones the body lacks, or no rig, is skipped with a warning. Belts, badges and pockets then fit the worn torso. Footwear stays unavailable on an installed body (an outfit brings its own). A generic catalog garment (`hoodie`) still validates on an installed body, as before, and is ignored with a warning, so a delta that switches a procedural look to an installed body does not fail.
8. **Conversion at install.** A model is often shipped as `.gltf` with 2K-4K PNG textures and more detail than the budgets (30,000 triangles, 15 MB per build) allow. `add-body` and `add-outfit` accept `.gltf`; the generator's `--convert` mode re-exports it as one `.glb` with textures shrunk (default 1024 px) and saved as JPEG unless they need alpha. `add-outfit` also decimates to about 18,000 triangles (`--max-triangles`; the body's head and eyes take about 7,000 and accessories a couple of thousand), which keeps bone weights.

9. **Lip-sync and blinking for an installed body with no mouth shape keys.** The generator already gives such a body a face overlay from a `<model>.face.json` sidecar (SPEC_BLENDER, "face overlay"): skin-coloured lids that close, and a nine-viseme mouth. `generator.py --face-landmarks` now writes that sidecar from the model's geometry, and `add-body` runs it (and `face-sidecar <body>` re-runs it; an existing sidecar is never overwritten without `--force`, since it may be hand-made). The eyes are the mesh whose material or texture is named for them (a pair, small, near the top of the figure, else the model is refused: a whole-body material that merely has "eye" in its name is not a face). The mouth is the lip crease in the face's side profile, the first local maximum after the upper-lip bulge, trusted only within the usual 55-75% of the way from eyes to chin and otherwise placed at 66.7%. For a realistic face the sidecar says `"patch": false` (keep the real lips; the mouth is drawn over them, since the default skin patch would erase them) and `"oval": true` (a round-edged dark mouth instead of a rectangle, with a strip of upper teeth along its top edge when it is open enough or the viseme is a teeth-together one). Landmarks are fractions of the body's own box (`body_bounds`), so an outfit worn over it (a hood) does not shift them.
10. **Gestures and skeletal idles on an installed body.** One mesh for the whole body used to get run poses but no arm gestures, because a mesh with run targets was skipped for gestures. It now gets the four gesture targets after the two run targets, and the overlay recognises a mesh with six targets as that pair (SPEC_OVERLAY). A rig with a head bone also gets idle, nod and shake clips baked on its own bones (`skeletal_clips` in the build and manifest), which the overlay plays by name; the generator's canonical clips are used only for generated bodies and rigs with no head bone.
11. **Holding a model back.** `<model>.hold` beside an installed `custom:` model keeps it on disk but out of the designer's options, and `validate_against_catalogs` refuses it, until `models release`. Used for models whose licence has not been checked.

12. **Relaxed arms.** Character bases are usually modelled in a T-pose and keep it as their rest pose, so the avatar idled with its arms out, and the gestures (which swing a limb sideways from hanging) flapped it up and down instead. An upper arm whose downward component is below `RELAX_IF_HANGING_LESS_THAN` (0.75, which is more than about 41 degrees off vertical: a T-pose arm is 0, a raised or bent arm about 0.6) is brought down to `RELAXED_ARM_DEG` (10) off vertical, with the forearm `RELAXED_ELBOW_DEG` (5) forward, and that is committed as the rest pose, after any outfit is worn, so everything hangs together; accessories, bounds and the morph poses are all measured from it. Arms that already hang, or are held forward (a mech's, at 0.8 and above), are left as built. The running poses also swing the arms against the legs, for a body that was relaxed this way. The original gesture poses (the overlay blends each arm between its "out" and "in" pose about once a second) were tuned on a mascot whose forearm swings in the plane of the body; on a person that is an elbow hinging sideways, with the upper arm dragging the shoulder and torso skin. A relaxed body gets talking gestures instead, identical for both arms: the elbow bends forward (about 72 degrees out, 28 in) and the upper arm moves only a little. A test pins the forward reach, which the older poses lacked entirely.

13. **Per-model pose fixes.** `<model>.pose.json` beside an installed model, `{"roll_bones": {"<bone>": degrees}}`, turns a bone about its own length in the rest pose, before the arms are relaxed and before any morph pose is captured. A hand modelled with its fingers turned outward makes an arm that bends read as folding the wrong way; `{"<hand bone>": 180}` turns it. A bone the model lacks is a warning, not a failure. The same file may hold `"face_yaw": degrees`, which turns a model that does not face the overlay's front (-Y) about the vertical axis before it is scaled (a robot modelled facing +X takes `-90`, one facing away takes `180`). Written by hand: it is a correction to one model, not something to derive.

14. **Animation libraries.** A pack of clips made for a rig (`models add-animations <file> --name <pack>` puts it in `assets/animation_library/`; Quaternius' CC0 Universal Animation Library is one) is worn by every installed body that has the core of the pack's bones (hips, both legs and both arms: `REQUIRED_BONES`; a body that lacks others, such as fingers, toes, clavicles or a spine joint, plays the clips without the tracks of the bones it lacks, which `wear_animation_library` drops): clips are keyed by bone name, so the generator (`plan_animation_library`, `wear_animation_library`) moves the clips that fill a role onto the body's armature as they are. Rotations do not depend on proportions; the hips' travel is scaled by the ratio of the two hip heights, and no other bone's length is touched. The roles and the clips that fill them are in `blender/animation_library.py` (pure Python, shared by the generator and `models list`): idle, talk, run, walk, idle_alt, celebrate, and sit_enter / sit_idle / sit_exit name the clips used, best first, and the build reports which clip fills which role as `library_clips` (a manifest field, sent to the overlay). A body that wears a library keeps its own rest pose, because the clips are relative to it, so it is not relaxed (decision 12), and it gets no morph-target run or gesture poses or baked head idles, which the clips replace; lip-sync and blinking are unaffected. A rig with other bone names ignores the library and keeps decisions 10 and 12. The overlay (SPEC_OVERLAY) plays the idle clip when nothing else is happening, the talk clip while speaking, the run clip while it crosses the screen, and, when bored, the folded-arms idle now and then; one clip at a time, and a timeline that is not playing is disabled, since all of them write the same bones. After a long idle (4-12 minutes with no speech, move or thinking) a body with all three sit clips sits down, plays the idle loop for 1-4 minutes, and stands up, and it also stands up first whenever it is about to move (to speak or think it stays seated and talks with `sit_talk` when the body has that clip, standing up after; without it, it stands up first for those too). The three have to be one set from one pack: `GroundSit_*` (sitting on the ground; only in Quaternius' Source pack, not the Standard one) if there is one, else the free `Sitting_*`, which are for a chair. For those the generator builds a plain chair (`build_sit_prop`: a seat, four legs and a low back) placed and sized from the posed body (the lowest vertex under the hips is where the seat's top goes; its front edge is just behind where the body stands when the sit-down begins, so it stands in front of the chair and steps back onto it) and named `SitProp`. The overlay cannot find a node by name (the loader leaves them unnamed), so the chair carries three morph targets, a collapse to nothing and two filler ones that make the count of three unique, and the controller drives the collapse weight; it is hidden after every load, grows in at the start of the sit-down and shrinks away partway through the stand-up. A move that is asked for while it sits waits for the stand-up and the chair going before it turns and runs (`requestStand`, held in `Main.qml`'s `slideTo`). The chair is left out of the bounds and the thumbnail. A rig with no `pelvis` / `thigh_l` / `calf_l` to place it from does not get the sit clips (with a warning), since it would sit on air. `models list` shows, per body, which libraries fit its rig and what they give (a library fits a body with the core of its bones, so mechs and mascots get nothing from the humanoid packs; a body that lacks some bones is shown with what stays still), and the status page's Animations tab shows the same per body. The idle clips are played 2.5x slower than made (`LIBRARY_TIME_SCALE`): the pack's idle breathes about 48 times a minute, which looked winded. The look's thumbnail is posed at the first frame of the idle clip, not the T-pose. Clips were chosen by looking at them: the pack's "Yes" is a thumbs-up, not a nod, and folded arms read as upset at any mood but bored, so neither is used as an idle variation.

A part that heat-map weighting leaves unweighted (loose low-poly pieces, common in downloaded robots) is bound whole to the bone nearest its centre, instead of being exported detached from the body.

## 3. Consequences

* Adding a model is: `add-body` or `add-accessory`, then it appears in the designer's options only where it works. `python -m brain.appearance.models list` shows the matrix.
* An accessory for a socket the rig lacks is rejected at validation, with the options, instead of being built in the wrong place. If a socket has no bone at build time (a body never inspected), the item is placed approximately and a warning is recorded.
* Chest, waist and hand placement on unusual bodies is approximate. A sidecar `offset`/`scale` corrects an individual accessory.
* A rigged accessory `.glb` is flattened to a rigid piece in its rest pose; `add-accessory` warns about it. Static meshes are best.
* `.caps.json` is a snapshot: re-run `add-body` after replacing a model file.
* An outfit is tied to one body: a female and a male outfit are two files under two folders, installed with the same name to be offered under it on each. Decimation and JPEG are lossy; keep the original download to re-install at other settings.
* The drawn mouth is a flat overlay: on a realistic face it reads as an open mouth at desktop-pet size, but it does not deform the lips or show teeth and tongue. Real mouth shape keys need topology that is cut at the lip line, which the free base meshes do not have. `add-body` prints the sidecar result; check the face in a render, since the mouth height is a proportion, not a measurement.
* An accessory placed over an outfit (a belt on a tunic) is fitted to the outfit's torso, and may be hidden by the outfit's own belts.

## 4. Alternatives considered

* **A hand-maintained compatibility table per body.** Goes stale the moment a model is added, and needs the user to know bone names.
* **Fitting generated garments onto an arbitrary installed body.** Needs per-mesh garment fitting; out of scope. Garments made for the body's own skeleton are supported (decision 7); other installed bodies keep their clothes.
* **Automatic weights for accessories.** Smears a hat across neighbouring bones. Rigid binding to one bone is exact.

## 5. Open questions

* Hand sockets assume the hand bone's origin is at the wrist; a grip offset may be needed per item (the sidecar `offset` covers it).
* The pack's modular parts (hood, pauldrons, arms, legs, feet) could be offered separately and mixed; today only whole outfits are installed.
* Shared skin textures: an outfit carries a copy of the body's skin texture for its hands, which adds about 0.5 MB per build.

15. **Quaternius is the supported source.** Bodies, outfits and animation libraries are made and tested for Quaternius content, and its bone names, T-pose rest pose and clip names are the convention everything else is matched against (see "Supported models" in SPEC_BLENDER). Content from another source needs two things before it works with the rest: an identifier (what kind of rig is this, from its bone names) and a converter to the Quaternius names and rest pose (for a body or an outfit: rename and re-skin; for a clip: retarget its rotations to the Quaternius bones). Until a source has both, its models are installed as `custom:` bodies with the generic, rig-reading features (decisions 1-12) and get no outfits or animation libraries. The per-body listing (`models list`) shows what each body can use.

16. **Mixamo converter.** Mixamo's skeleton has the same 65 bones as the Quaternius one in the same arrangement (bar a tip bone, `HeadTop_End`, and Quaternius' `root`) and the same T-pose, so it is converted, not rebuilt (`blender/mixamo_convert.py`; the name map and the clip processing are in `blender/animation_library.py`). **A body** (`add-body <file>.fbx`): the bones and vertex groups are renamed to the Quaternius names, each bone is rolled like the Quaternius bone (a clip turns a bone about its own axes, so a body renamed only crosses its legs under Quaternius clips; checked by eye), `root` is added above the hips, and the FBX's centimetre scale and rotation are applied; the result goes through the usual `add-body` steps. **Clips** (`add-animations <files>.fbx --name <pack>`; Blender 5's `wm.fbx_import`, the old importer fails headless): every frame is retargeted onto a Quaternius skeleton as the change in each bone's world rotation from its rest pose, the hips' travel is scaled by hip height, and the pack is written as an ordinary animation library (decision 14), keys thinned to 0.25 degrees. Per clip (`mixamo_clip_defaults`, overridable with `--options`): `trim`, `loop` (the end eased back to the start), `in_place`, `variants` (a `_Low` and a `_High` copy at 0.55x and 1.4x the movement, slower and faster, about the first pose: how eager a greeting is), `extract` (part of a clip as a clip of its own: the first half of Angry has the hips pushed out sideways every 2.7 s, which loops cleanly from frame 57 to 217 as `Angry_Hip_Thrust`; that sideways travel is the motion, so it is not stripped), and `seat`: a seated clip is moved to where the sit clips seat the body and is eased in from, and back to, the sit pose, so playing it from the sit loop does not jump. Its hips go the whole way to our seat height and its legs are re-solved with two-bone IK (`Host.plant_feet`) so the feet stay where the clip puts them (kept above the floor, the knee bending the way the clip bends it, the foot turned as the clip turns it); `plant_feet: false` in the options goes back to moving the hips only. `mirror` (on by default): the clip is made a second time with left and right swapped (`<clip>_Mirror`, without intensity variants of its own, which cost size, so a `.high` or `.low` clip is not also mirrored; `mirror_main: false` mirrors only the extracted parts of a long clip, as for Angry, where only the hip thrust is mirrored; each bone's world change from rest is reflected in the plane that splits the body, and the hips' sideways travel flips), and the overlay plays one of the two at random, so a gesture is not always made with the same hand. Mixamo's modifiers (Enthusiasm, Wave...) are baked into the file and cannot be read back (the .fbx carries no trace of them); the intensity variants are made here instead, and the brain's `play_gesture` carries an `intensity` (0..1, from valence, energy and stress, 0.5 at rest) that the overlay turns into the low, normal or high clip. Floor clips (sleeping, working out): Stand_Up and Burpee start from a push-up position and Sleeping_Idle / Laying_Idle are on the floor, so the clips are joined by easing, in `finish_clips` (pure Python): `ease_from` / `ease_to` ease a clip in from, or out to, the first pose of another (`@stand` is the standing idle's first pose, `stand_reference`, so Stand_Up ends where the idle begins), `blend_ref` does both ends to one pose, and `reverse` plays a clip backwards: **Lie_Down** is Stand_Up reversed, eased from the idle in and into the sleeping pose out. **Workout_Burpee** and **Workout_Crunch** are derived clips (`compose`; `mixamo_derived_clips`): lie down, the exercise (2 burpees, 5 crunches), get up, joined by easing and from and to the idle, so one clip takes it from standing to standing again. The `in_place` option is on for them, so nothing travels and the next clip does not jump. The crunch is on its back, so the roll from the push-up position is the easing's. Roles added: workout, workout_floor, lie_down, sleep, sleep_alt, wake, wake_slow, idle_look, idle_sad and the floor sit set (floor_sit_enter / _idle / _exit, which Ground_Sitting_Idle is made into: `hold` derived clips that ease from the standing idle's pose to the seated one and back, over 36 samples) (the overlay: SPEC_OVERLAY). **Options.** A role can have several clips (`LIBRARY_EXTRAS`: talk has Talking and Talking_1 beside the idle-talking loop, greet has Waving and Waving_1, idle_look has Looking and Looking_Around): the first is the role's key in `library_clips`, the others are `<role>.alt1`, `.alt2`, each with its own `.low` / `.high` / `.mirror` copies, and the overlay picks one at random. **Sleeping.** The sleep role is Sleeping_Idle_1 (she breathes on her back) and sleep_alt is `Sleeping_Roll`, made from the first 6.5 s of Sleeping_Idle: that clip ends with her turning right over (150 degrees by its ninth second), which, looped, was undone in 0.4 s, a barrel roll. `Sleeping_Roll` is the restless start of it, slowed by 1.5 and played out and back along the same path (`ping_pong`, with the time eased so she is still at the turn rather than bouncing off it): about 50 degrees at the turn, 19 s, ending in the pose it began in, which is the first pose of Sleeping_Idle_1, so the loop carries on without a jump. The overlay plays it 2.5-5 minutes after she lies down and then every 3-8 minutes. Laying and Laying_Idle (the position she rolls into, 133 degrees from the sleeping pose) have no role: eased to the sleeping pose they were a second barrel roll. A role that a second library also has takes that library's clips as more options (`merge_picks`), so the pack's own talk clip stays one of them. Waving and Waving_1 are converted with intensity variants and eased from and to the idle, as Looking and Looking_Around are (`blend_ref: @stand`). Roles added: greet (+ `.low` / `.high`), angry, hip_thrust, sit_alt (a seated variation, played now and then while it sits), sit_talk (Sitting_Talking, played in a loop while it speaks from the chair: it does not stand up to talk or to think, it talks sitting down and stands up once it has finished; a move still has it stand first. A chair is needed, so the floor sit has no such clip). `angry` and `hip_thrust` are also gestures the companion can do.

17. **Sketchfab importer.** A Sketchfab download (a .glb whose `asset.generator` starts with `Sketchfab`) is the author's model re-exported by Sketchfab, and installing it as it comes does not work: the author's centimetre scale sits on wrapper nodes above the model; every node name has a uniqueness suffix (`CC_Base_Head_038`); Blender's importer adds a helper bone above each scaled bone (`..._scaleCompensation`, 99 of them on a 102-bone rig, so that the sockets landed on helpers); the file carries the motion it was exported with; a model of half a million triangles is normal, and its meshes have shape keys, which a mesh cannot be decimated with, so it would stay far over the 30,000-triangle build budget; and the importer's own bone-display sphere (a two-metre `Icosphere`) was re-exported with the model by the convert step (a bug for any rigged model, fixed in `run_convert`). `add-body` therefore recognises a Sketchfab file and cleans it first (`blender/sketchfab_convert.py`, rules in `blender/sketchfab.py`, pure Python): the wrapper transforms are baked into a one-metre-scale, Z-up skeleton and meshes with nothing above them; the helper bones and `_rootJoint` are removed, the bones under them re-parented; the suffix is taken off the bones and vertex groups (one suffix only: `spine_01_x_04` is `spine_01_x`; a name that would then be taken keeps Sketchfab's); shape keys, the baked motion, the empties and the display sphere are dropped; loose parts hanging from one bone are skinned to it; textures are shrunk (JPEG) and the meshes decimated to the body budget in the same pass (the small parts keep their detail, so the ratio is taken on the rest); and the file is made again with halved textures while it is over 8 MB (a model with 57 materials has 57 textures). The cleaned file then goes through the usual `add-body` steps (inspection, sockets, face sidecar). **Licence.** The author, title, source and licence are in `asset.extras`; they are recorded in `<model>.credits.json` beside the installed model (for bodies, outfits and accessories), shown by `models list` and the status page's Animations tab, and what the licence asks is printed on install: CC-BY and CC-BY-SA need the author credited wherever the model is shown or shared, CC-BY-NC is for non-commercial use, CC-BY-ND forbids sharing a modified version (and we always shrink and re-rig, so it is for the user's own use only), and a licence that is not Creative Commons is flagged to be read. Nothing is refused: the user decides. The models themselves are never part of the repository. **Scope.** This is an importer, not a skeleton converter: a Sketchfab rig is whatever its author made (Character Creator's `CC_Base_*`, Auto-Rig Pro's `*_stretch_l`...), so the Quaternius animation libraries do not fit it as it is (decision 15; decision 18 converts the two known families) and `models list` says so; a rig that is no known family plays the generator's own motion for imported rigs (the hop and the baked limb poses, the head idles), for which the limb finder now also knows Auto-Rig Pro's `thigh_stretch` / `leg_stretch` / `arm_stretch` / `forearm_stretch`. The converter that makes such a body play the libraries is decision 18. Checked on two downloads: a 53 MB Character Creator soldier (510,358 triangles, CC-BY) became 24,000 triangles and 2.3 MB and builds with no warnings, and a 254 MB mecha (57 textures, CC-BY-NC) became 25,000 triangles at 512 px textures and builds at 12 MB with the run and gesture poses baked (which needed the budget of decision 8 raised to 15 MB).
18. **Rig converter.** A body of a known rig family plays the Quaternius animation libraries through a conversion (`blender/rig_convert.py`; the family detection and the bone maps are in `blender/rigs.py`, pure Python). Two families are known, the two the Sketchfab downloads came with: Character Creator (`CC_Base_*`) and Auto-Rig Pro (`*_stretch_l`, `root_x`). Mixamo's decision 16 renames bone for bone because its skeleton is the Quaternius one in all but names; these rigs are not: their bones differ in number (five spine joints, twist and facial bones, a hips bone with a pelvis bone under it), in the way each bone is turned (a clip turns a bone about its own axes, and rolls differ from tool to tool), and in the rest pose (Character Creator's arms hang; the Quaternius clips are the change from a T-pose). So a library is not worn as it is: it is **retargeted** onto the body. **Body** (`add-body` does it for a `custom:` body of a known family, unless `--no-rig`): the bones that have a Quaternius equivalent are renamed (`CC_Base_Hip` is `pelvis`, `CC_Base_Waist` `spine_01`, `arm_stretch_l` `upperarm_l`...; five spine joints give the first, the middle and the last), and every other bone keeps its name and follows its parent; the body is turned to face as the Quaternius characters do (its left along +X); and, if its arms are not within 12 degrees of straight out, they are posed into a T-pose, the meshes deformed to it and the pose made the rest pose (so shape keys, which cannot be, are gone: decision 17). **Libraries** (every installed library, when the body is installed; a library added later, when it is): each clip a role uses (`wanted_clips`, not the other forty) has its frames walked on the library's skeleton and the change of each bone's world rotation from its rest pose applied to the body's bone as the change from its own (so the bones' own axes do not matter; only that both rest in the same pose, which the T-pose ensures); a bone with no equivalent stays at its place under its parent; the hips' travel is scaled by how tall the two hips stand; the keys are thinned as the Mixamo converter does, and the first is at time 0 (a clip that starts later shows the rest pose until it, see decision 16). The result is the body's own library, `assets/animation_library_body/<body>/<library>.glb`, and `<body>.rig.json` says the body is converted: the generator (`plan_animation_library`, `library_folder`) then plays those libraries, not the shared ones, and `models list` says so. A body lacking Quaternius bones (a four-fingered mech has no index finger) plays them without those tracks (decision 14). Checked on the two Sketchfab downloads: the soldier (53 of its 101 bones mapped, arms posed out) and the mecha (47 of 73): idle, talk, run, walk, dance, sit on a chair and the Mixamo clips play on both, and a library is retargeted in about ten seconds. **Not covered:** a rig of another family has no map (`rig_family` says none, the body is installed as it is, and plays the generator's own motion); a skeleton whose bones are not named for what they are (`Bone.001`) cannot be mapped by name; a retargeted clip does not account for different limb proportions (the feet may slide or sink a little on a body with short legs, and the hand may not reach where the clip's does); and the seat of a chair-sit clip is the library's, which may sit a body of other proportions a little high or low on its chair (not measured).
19. **Model bundles (export and import).** The models are in the deploy directory, on the machine's own disk, and nothing but the nightly rsync of the whole directory (`deploy/backup_state.sh`) copies them anywhere. Starting a second install (a Windows machine, with a fresh companion and the models of the first; and a third that cannot reach the NAS, by thumb drive) needs the models alone, carried as one file, in a form that cannot half-arrive. Zipping the directory does not do that: it takes the live database (copied mid-write it can be torn; the backup script uses SQLite's backup API for that reason), the Linux binaries in `tools/`, the built looks and the voices, which are not models, and it checks nothing on the other side. `models export <file.zip>` and `models import <file.zip>` (`brain/appearance/bundle.py`, standard library only: no Blender to export) do it. **What is in a bundle:** the files under `assets/` in `base_templates`, `custom_templates`, `outfit_models`, `wardrobe` (accessories), `animation_library`, and `animation_library_body` (the libraries retargeted onto converted bodies, decision 18), restricted to `.glb`, `.blend` and `.json` and never a `.hold` marker (state); each model's sockets, face, pose, rig and credits sidecars go with it. **What is not:** the database, the look history and built looks, voices, tools, and the catalogs (the repository seeds them, so they come with it; an export says so if they differ from the seed). A bundle therefore makes a *fresh companion* with the models and nothing of the old one. **The format** (`bundle.json`, format 1): a version, when it was made, whether it includes the derived libraries, the Blender and repository revision it was made with (for diagnosis only), the models by kind, and `files`: `{path: {sha256, size}}`, the map the remote worker's file transfer needs too (ADR_REMOTE_MODEL_PROCESSING). A `CREDITS.txt` lists every credited model with its licence and what it asks (attribution, non-commercial, no derivatives), so a bundle that is passed on carries its obligations, and an import prints them. **Export** refuses what could not be a file on Windows (a reserved name such as `CON`, a character `<>:"\|?*`, a trailing dot or space, a path of more than 150 characters, two names that differ only in case) and writes to a temporary file that replaces the target only when complete; it warns when the bundle would not fit a FAT32 drive (4 GB). **Import** trusts nothing in the bundle: every name must be a relative path under a model folder with a model suffix (no `..`, no drive, no backslash, nothing outside `assets/<model folder>/`), every file must be in the manifest and every manifest entry in the bundle, sizes must match, the total unpacked size is capped (8 GB), and every file is unpacked to a staging directory and checked against its hash before anything is moved into place, so a damaged or altered bundle writes nothing. A file already there with the same contents is left; one with different contents is a conflict and is left too, unless `--replace`; a name that differs only in case from one there is a conflict (a Windows install cannot hold both). `--dry-run` reads and verifies the whole bundle and writes nothing. **Derived libraries:** they are in the bundle by default, so that a machine without Blender can use its converted bodies at once; `export --no-derived` leaves them out (16 MB of a 35 MB bundle at the time) and `import --regenerate` makes them again (Blender needed, same as `add-animations`; use it when the Blender version differs from the one that made them). An import of a bundle with a converted body and no libraries for it says so. Checked on the models of this install: 66 files, 67.7 MB unpacked and 34.9 MB zipped, exported in two seconds, and imported into an empty directory as byte-identical files; a second import changed nothing; a file with one bit flipped was refused. **A look to start with** (`export --include-look [--look-version N]`): the models alone give an install nothing to show until Blender has built a look from them, and the first Windows box, and the one with no NAS, may not have Blender yet. The bundle can therefore also carry one finished look: its built `.glb` (checked against its record's hash at export, so a damaged or pruned file stops the export), its manifest record, and its picture, as the three files `look/look.glb`, `look/entry.json` and `look/look.png` (no other name is accepted under `look/`). An import into an install that has **no `manifest.json`** sets it up as a first publish does, with the version number reset: the file into `assets/` and `data/current/`, the picture into `looks/`, then the manifest with that one record as **version 1 and the current look**; the companion starts with it, the overlay plays it (its clips, outfit and poses are in the file, so nothing needs Blender to be seen), and the next look she is asked for is version 2, built by Blender once it is installed and configured. An install that already has looks is left alone (the bundle's look is reported as not used): this never rewrites a companion's history. A bad look (a file that is not the one its record describes, a record that is not a look record) is refused before anything is written. The look carried is a build of the models in the same bundle, so what it was made from is there for the next build. **Not covered:** the companion's memory and personality (`state.db`; a deliberate choice for a fresh companion, and `backup_state.sh` already snapshots it), the rest of the look history (the other looks rebuild from their specs), voices (a companion's own), and the Windows port itself (Blender's path, the scripts and the overlay build are separate work).
